Research

What we study

The lab's work is organized around three themes. Each is led by a faculty co-director and supported by graduate and undergraduate researchers.

Theme 01 · Dr. Laurel Powell

Intelligent Educational Analytics

We use data mining and machine learning to understand student learning and risk. That includes early-warning models, engagement analysis, and interactive tools that help instructors read and respond to what the data shows.

  • Predicting and reducing academic risk
  • Analyzing learning behavior and engagement
  • Dashboards and visualization for instructors

In practice

Recent lab work applies unsupervised machine learning and interactive storytelling to make patterns of student success and risk understandable to educators.

In practice

Members study how tools like generative AI change the student experience in programming courses, and design curricula that build AI literacy across disciplines.

Theme 02 · Dr. Aileen Benedict

AI Literacy & Learning Tools

We design AI-based tools that support learning and study how students develop the ability to use AI thoughtfully. The emphasis is on tools that strengthen student thinking rather than replace it.

  • Human-centered AI tools for learning
  • AI literacy curriculum and assessment
  • Metacognition and reflection in AI-supported study
Theme 03 · Dr. Yash Tadimalla

Trustworthy AI in Education

Data-driven methods should produce results educators can rely on. We study transparency, reliability, and accountability so that analytics and AI in education are dependable and understandable to the people who use them.

  • Transparency and reliability in educational models
  • Analysis of active learning and classroom climate
  • Responsible and accountable use of AI

In practice

Lab research examines how active-learning practices affect student outcomes in introductory computing courses.

Externally funded research

Lab research connects to externally funded projects led by the director, Dr. Mohsen Dorodchi.

National Science Foundation · S-STEM

I-PASS: Improving the Persistence and Success of Students in Computer Science

Principal Investigator: Dr. Mohsen Dorodchi (with co-PIs across UNC Charlotte). A multi-year NSF award (approx. $1M) providing scholarships, mentoring, and improved introductory-course instruction for computer science students.

More NSF-supported work

The director's research spans NSF-funded projects on computing education, student success, and learning analytics. A complete list is available through the NSF award database and his Google Scholar profile.
